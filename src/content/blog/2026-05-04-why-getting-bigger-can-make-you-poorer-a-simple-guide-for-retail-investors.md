Most people believe something simple:

If a company becomes bigger… it must be doing better.

But that’s not always true.

Sometimes, a company grows in size —  
but the **owners actually become poorer**.

***

## **The Ice Cream Shop + Dairy Deal**

Let’s keep it simple.

Imagine you own a **high-quality ice cream shop**.

### **Your shop:**
- Total value: ₹10,00,000  
- Yearly Profit: ₹2,00,000 (20% return)  
- You own: 100%  

It’s profitable, efficient, and trusted.

---

Now there’s a **nearby dairy business**.

### **The dairy:**
- Lower quality  
- Slower growth  
- Deal Value: ₹10,00,000  
- Yearly Profit: ₹50,000 (5% return)

---

### **The deal**

You decide to “merge” to expand.

But instead of paying cash:

👉 You give **50% ownership of your shop**  
👉 In exchange for **100% of the dairy**

### **After the deal:**
- You own:
  - 50% of your original shop → ₹1,00,000 profit share  
  - 100% of the dairy → ₹50,000 profit  

👉 Total profit now = **₹1,50,000**

---

### Let’s compare:

Before:
- Profit = ₹2,00,000  

After:
- Profit = ₹1,50,000  

### What happened?

> You gave away half of a high-return business (₹1,00,000 loss)  
> and received a low-return business in exchange (₹50,000 gain)

👉 Net result: **₹50,000 loss every year**



### Important detail:

Both businesses are priced at ₹10 lakh in the deal…  
even though one is clearly stronger.

---

> Your business became bigger.  
> But your income became smaller.

## **What You Actually Gave Away**

Before:
- You owned a **strong business fully**

After:
- You own **half of a strong business**
- Plus a **weaker business**

👉 Simple truth:

> You didn’t just “buy” a dairy.  
> You **sold half your best business**.

***

## **Why This Can Make You Poorer**

Let’s look deeper.

### **If both businesses were equally strong**

You break even.

### **If the new business is weaker**

Now:
- Your strong business → reduced ownership  
- Your new business → lower returns  

👉 Overall:
**Your total earning power drops**

> Getting bigger does not mean getting richer.

***

## **Now Add One More Twist**

Imagine this:

Instead of giving 50%,  
you give **60% of your shop**.

Now:
- You no longer control your own business  
- Decisions may be taken that don’t favor you  

For example:
- Cost-cutting  
- Quality reduction  
- Long-term decisions sacrificed for short-term gains  

👉 You may lose both:
- Control  
- Quality of your original business  

![Dilution effect visual simplified](blog_article_image_2_growth_of_company_may_refuce_profit_ohpfxu)

## **What About Real Companies?**

Companies don’t use “shops”.

They use **shares**.

When a company wants to buy another business, it can:

- Pay cash  
OR  
- Issue new shares  

---

### What happens when new shares are issued?

Imagine a pizza with 8 slices.

You own 2 slices.

Now the company creates 2 extra slices to make a deal.

👉 Total slices = 10  

You still have 2 slices…  
but your share is now smaller.

![Pizza Slice Visual of Equity Dilution](blog_article_image_3_bigger_company_does_not_always_mean_better_vbrbql)

> More shares. Less ownership.

---

### One more important point:

If total profit does not increase enough after the deal,  
👉 each slice earns less.

This is called a drop in **earnings per share (EPS)**.


> Same pizza. More slices. Smaller share per person.

***

## **Another Subtle Warning Sign**

Sometimes companies may change how they report performance.


### Example:

Earlier:
- “Net Profit”  

Now:
- “Adjusted Profit”  

Adjusted numbers may:
- Exclude certain costs  
- Present a smoother picture  



👉 Ask:

> Why is the old number no longer shown?  
> What might not be visible here?



## **Why Trust Matters**

Even one poor decision can:

- Reduce investor confidence  
- Lower how the company is valued  



### Simple truth:

> Trust takes years to build… and one decision to damage.


## **A Simple Checklist for You**

Before getting impressed by headlines like:

**“Company X acquires Company Y”**

Ask:

- What is the company **giving away**?
- Is the new business actually **better**?
- Is ownership being reduced?
- Are financial numbers being changed or replaced?
