Around 1977, many investors began to look at businesses differently—not just by profit, but by how efficiently that profit was created. For the modern Indian reader, these observations help separate companies that are truly successful from those that are just growing in size.

Here are three simple ways to look at the logic of a business.

***

## **1. The Earnings Illusion**
It is common to see people celebrate when a company reports its highest-ever "Earnings Per Share" (EPS). However, if a company has to keep reinvesting more and more money into the business every year, the total profit can rise over time-much like a savings account—but only if that money continues to earn good returns.

**The Observation:** A rise in profit isn't always a sign of a better business. It is often useful to check how much new money was needed to achieve that extra profit.

**In Practice:** This dynamic can often be observed in capital-intensive industries where additional capital is required to sustain growth. In these cases, "Record Profits" can sometimes be a distraction.

> **THE BOTTOM LINE:**
> **If the money needed to run the business grows faster than the profit it makes, the business is getting 'heavier,' not necessarily better.**

***

## **2. The Profit Speedometer (ROE)**
Return on Equity (ROE) is a simple way to see how hard every rupee in the business is working. 

**The Observation:** One way to look at a company is to see if it is earning a healthy return on the money it keeps, rather than just getting physically larger (like adding more offices or staff) without actually making more money.

**In Practice:** One can study how certain companies manage to keep their returns on capital steady over time without needing to borrow more money.

> **THE BOTTOM LINE:**
> **A high and steady ROE usually indicates a business engine that is efficient. It produces more 'output' for every liter of 'fuel' (capital) put in.**

***

## **3. The Economic Wind**
The industry a company operates in often plays a bigger role than individual effort alone. 

**The Observation:** Some businesses face a "Headwind"—like riding a bicycle into a storm. Others have a "Tailwind," where the wind pushes them forward even if they aren't pedaling hard.

**In Practice:** This is visible when a whole sector (like Digital Tech or Clean Energy) grows because the world is changing, making it easier for every company in that space to succeed.

> **THE BOTTOM LINE:**
> **This framework highlights how capital efficiency can influence long-term outcomes in business performance. Progress is simply more efficient when the industry logic is on your side.**
