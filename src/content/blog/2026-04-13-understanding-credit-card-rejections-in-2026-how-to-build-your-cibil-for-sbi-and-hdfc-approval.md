Got rejected for a credit card? You are not alone.

In 2026, Indian banks have tightened their approval filters significantly. SBI, HDFC, ICICI — all of them now run algorithmic checks that can reject an application in seconds, often without telling you exactly why.

A rejection does not mean you are bad with money. It means your current data profile did not match the bank's internal criteria at this moment. That is fixable — but only if you understand what the criteria actually are.

---

## What is a Good CIBIL Score in India?

CIBIL scores run from 300 to 900. Here is what each range may mean for credit card approvals in 2026:

**750 – 900 (Excellent)**
- Generally higher approval chances — most premium cards may be accessible

**700 – 749 (Good)**
- Moderate approval chances — entry and mid-tier cards possible

**650 – 699 (Fair)**
- Low approval chances — most banks are likely to decline

**300 – 649 (Poor)**
- Very low approval chances — secured cards may be the only option

For SBI SimplyCLICK, HDFC Millennia, or most premium rewards cards, a score of 750 or above is generally what banks look for. Below that, many applications are filtered automatically before a human reviews them.

---

## Why Did Your Credit Card Application Get Rejected?

Most rejections from major Indian banks fall into these categories:

### 1. CIBIL Score Below the Bank's Threshold
Every bank maintains its own internal minimum. For premium cards, 750 is a commonly observed floor. Some banks may require 780+ for their top-tier products.

### 2. Employment Profile
Banks typically verify employment stability before approving cards. If you are a freelancer, gig worker, working at a small startup, or self-employed without filed ITRs — banks may find it difficult to confirm your income reliability, regardless of how much you actually earn.

### 3. Over-Leveraging
If you already carry multiple loans (home loan, personal loan, car EMIs) alongside existing credit cards, banks calculate your **debt-to-income ratio**. Too many existing obligations may trigger a rejection even with a reasonable CIBIL score.

### 4. The Hard Inquiry Trap
Every rejected application creates a **hard inquiry** on your CIBIL report, which may lower your score slightly. Many people make this worse by immediately applying to another bank after a rejection — triggering another hard inquiry, another potential drop, another rejection.

**If you were rejected today: stop applying immediately.** Consider waiting at least 3 to 6 months before your next application.

## How to Check Your CIBIL Score for Free

You are entitled to one free CIBIL report per year directly from the official source.

**Official free check:** Go to [cibil.com](https://www.cibil.com) → click "Get Your Free CIBIL Score" → create an account with your PAN card details.

**Important:** Consider checking your score only through official RBI-authorised bureaus — CIBIL, Experian, CRIF, or Equifax. Third-party apps that offer "free credit score checks" may trigger soft inquiries or share your data with lenders without your full awareness.

**How often to check:** Once every 3 months is generally sufficient.

## How to Work Towards Improving Your CIBIL Score

There is no shortcut. But improvement tends to be more predictable when you follow consistent steps over time.

### In the Next 30 Days
*   Pay every existing EMI and credit card bill on time, in full — not just the minimum due
*   Check your CIBIL report for possible errors (wrong account details, closed loans still showing as active) and consider disputing them directly with CIBIL online
*   Stop all new credit applications immediately

### In 3 to 6 Months
*   Try to keep your **credit utilisation below 30%** — if your card limit is ₹1,00,000, aim to keep your outstanding balance below ₹30,000
*   If you have a card you rarely use, consider making one small purchase on it monthly and paying it off immediately — this may help keep the account active and build history
*   Avoid closing old credit cards where possible — longer credit history can be a positive factor

### In 6 to 12 Months
*   With consistent on-time payments and low utilisation, many people report a 50 to 100 point improvement over this period — though individual results vary
*   At around 700+, entry-level cards from IDFC, Kotak, or Axis may become accessible
*   At around 750+, premium cards from SBI and HDFC may come within reach

## Managing the Hard Inquiry Risk

Every time you apply for a card and get rejected, your CIBIL score may take a small hit from a hard inquiry.

*   **Consider this:** If you have been rejected by SBI or HDFC today, do not apply for another card immediately
*   **A reasonable approach:** Wait at least 3 to 6 months. Use this time to track your expenses and ensure all current bills are paid on time
