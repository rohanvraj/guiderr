But for Rohan Raj, the answer has never needed explanation.

He has been riding since he was 18. Not occasionally. Not recreationally. But consistently-across years, across states, across moods.

## **Where It Started**

Long before platforms and algorithms, the fascination was already there.

As a kid, Rohan would download motorcycle videos from the internet and watch them with his brother Nakul-back when YouTube wasn’t even a thing. There was something about machines in motion that stayed with him.

At 18, he got his first bike: a **black Pulsar 150 DTS-i**.

It wasn’t just a bike. He named her Black Lightning. It was the beginning.

He learnt everything on it.

The early rides were to **Lavasa and Mahabaleshwar**—roads full of curves, elevation changes, and mistakes waiting to happen. That’s where he began to understand riding, not just do it.

Balance. Control. Lean. Timing.

> Those roads quietly taught him the physics of riding.

Back in Pune, the learning continued in a different form-traffic, unpredictability, constant movement. City riding sharpened awareness in a way highways never could.

Over time, one belief stayed constant:

> **Anyone can go fast. Knowing how to slow down is where real skill shows.**

Then life paused the journey for a while.

Years passed.

And in 2019, he returned-with more clarity.

This time, he chose the **KTM Duke 390** what he calls the *Iron Horse*.

Not impulsively. Not emotionally.

But because he understood what he needed.

It was the right machine at the right time-for one clear goal:

To ride across India, and on a powerful bike.

And that’s exactly what he did. What started as a machine became something quieter, deeper. A rhythm.

## **The Map is the Memory**

Over time, the map of India stopped being geography. It became experience.

He has ridden through:
- The dense forests of Madhya Pradesh, where the air feels heavier and quieter  
- The long, unfamiliar stretches of Odisha, Chhattisgarh, and Jharkhand  
- The coastal roads leading into Goa, where the wind carries salt and ease  
- The stark deserts of Rajasthan, where distance feels endless  
- The India–Pakistan border at Nadabet, where stillness replaces noise  
- The white silence of the Rann of Kutch  
- The fertile plains of Punjab  
- The mountains of Himachal and Jammu & Kashmir  
- And the full Ladakh circuit, where roads test both machine and mind

## **The Night at Chang La**

Not every ride is smooth. Some stay with you for different reasons.

One evening in October, while crossing **Chang La Pass** in Ladakh, things changed quickly.

It was getting dark. The temperature had dropped to nearly **-10°C**.  
There were no tourists. No vehicles. No network.

Just silence.

![Chang La Pass Ride](changla_pass_ktm_motorcycle_duke_390_ride_rohan_raj_wtab70)
*Chang La Pass — October evening, -10°C. No traffic. No signal. Just the road ahead.*

Rohan was riding with his wife as pillion.

The road was empty. The air was thin. No sound except the engine and the wind. Every movement of the bike demanded precision.

At that altitude, even a small mistake doesn’t give you a second chance.

> There are moments on the road where confidence becomes responsibility.

Extreme cold starts to slow your reactions. Hands stiffen. Visibility drops.  
And yet, you keep moving—not fast, not aggressively—but carefully.

Because stopping is not always an option.

They crossed that stretch alone.

No noise. No rush. Just focus.

> Some rides don’t test your skill. They test your composure.

That night stayed with him.

Not as fear—but as respect.

Each landscape asks for something different. Patience. Awareness. Respect.

And over time, the rider changes too.

## **Why He Rides**

People often ask him the same question:
**“Don’t you get tired?”**

The answer is always the same.

No.

Because riding does not drain him. It resets him.

There is a clarity that comes somewhere between long highways and silent stretches. A kind of stillness that doesn’t exist in routine life.

> Movement is healing.

It sharpens attention. It clears noise. It brings you back to yourself.
